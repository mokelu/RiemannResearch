The company name is: Riemann.
The product is a Research Notebook.
The website is riemannresearch.space
Though it is somewhat empty for now.

Imagine you ask AI:
> “Should I get a dog?”

AI says:
> “Dogs are fun, but they need a lot of care. If you have enough time, getting a dog could be a good idea.”

We deliberately tell the AI:
> “When you answer, don't just write plain text. Break what you're saying into sentences, put a tag on each one, and write down how the sentences connect to each other.”

The AI does two jobs, and both are the AI's invention:
1. It picks the tag for each sentence.
2. It picks the connection between two sentences and names it.

Because normally, the computer just gets:

```text
Dogs are fun, but they need a lot of care. If you have enough time, getting a dog could be a good idea.
```

But now it gets:

```text
QUESTION
Should I get a dog?

FACT
Dogs need a lot of care.

    supports →

ASSUMPTION
I have enough time.

    implies →

CONCLUSION
Getting a dog could be a good idea.
```

Now imagine you ask:

> “What if I don't have enough time?”

The AI can select the ASSUMPTION and continue:

```text
OBJECTION
I don't have enough time.

        ↓

CONSEQUENCE
Taking care of the dog could be difficult.
```

So the computer is no longer holding only:

```text
paragraph
paragraph
paragraph
```

It is holding:

```text
             QUESTION
            ↙        ↘
FACT                           OBJECTION
   ↓ supports →                    ↓ implies →
ASSUMPTION                     CONSEQUENCE
   ↓ implies →
CONCLUSION
```

That is the basic Riemann Research Notebook idea:

> The AI doesn't just write words — it also writes the structure underneath them. Riemann keeps that structure, so the computer holds the graph, not just the prose. And Riemann then lets the user traverse, interact, edit, and understand that graph better.

Its like what AI chatUI was supposed to be.
