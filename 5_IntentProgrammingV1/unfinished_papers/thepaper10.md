# Chapter 10 — Riemann 1 and the Future Vision

## 10.1 Riemann 1

**Riemann 1 is the first prototype of the linguistic event-driven model described in this work.**

Its purpose is to demonstrate that software components can be treated as computational selves that receive intent, respond to events, communicate with one another, and act within a shared World.

The basic flow is:

```text
Intent
  ↓
AI
  ↓
Symphony
  ↓
Maxi
  ↓
Finite Selves
  ↓
World
```

The prototype is built around the idea that the software world is not made only of passive components.

A Button can have a Self.

A Chart can have a Self.

A Spreadsheet can have a Self.

These selves can receive instructions and continue acting after the original AI interaction has ended.

Riemann 1 therefore provides a concrete environment in which **Intent Programming, Symphony, Maxi, FiniteSelf, and LEDEN** can operate together.

---

## 10.2 The Microsoft Future Vision

Microsoft's **Productivity Future Vision (2011)** presented a vision of computing in which technology would become increasingly contextual, connected, and capable of working on a person's behalf. Microsoft described the vision as a future in which technology moved beyond passive tools and became a more active assistant, with context playing a much larger role.

The video showed a world filled with connected displays, devices, collaboration systems, natural interfaces, and intelligent assistance.

The important idea was not any individual device.

It was the idea of a world in which computing is present throughout everyday life and can respond to what people are doing and trying to accomplish.

---

## 10.3 From Vision to Programming Model

Riemann 1 takes this idea in a different direction.

The question is no longer only:

> **What if technology could work on our behalf?**

The question becomes:

> **What if the things inside the software world could themselves participate in computation?**

In the Microsoft vision, technology appears increasingly active.

In the Riemann model, that activity becomes a programming primitive.

```text
Button
Chart
Spreadsheet
Workspace
AI
```

can become computational selves.

They can receive instructions.

They can react to events.

They can communicate.

They can perform tasks.

They can continue operating without continuous supervision from the original AI interaction.

---

## 10.4 A New Kind of Computing Environment

This leads to a broader vision of software.

Instead of applications being collections of passive objects controlled by a central program, an application can become a **world populated by computational selves**.

Intent is expressed.

AI produces a Symphony.

Maxi coordinates the Symphony.

Finite selves perform their parts.

LEDEN allows events and linguistic instructions to remain active within the world.

The World records and exposes the resulting state.

This provides a possible foundation for a new type of operating environment: **Abracadabra aiOS**.

The objective is not simply to make existing software easier to control.

It is to change the underlying model of interaction between people, software, and the things software contains.

---

## 10.5 The Key Idea

> **Riemann 1 is a prototype for a future in which software is not merely a collection of tools, but a living computational world whose components can receive intent, communicate, respond to events, and act on their own.**
