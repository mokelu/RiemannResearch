# Chapter 4 — Symphony

## 4.1 What Is Symphony?

**Symphony is the structured representation of an intent that coordinates multiple computational selves.**

It is the program that describes how different selves participate in an intended outcome.

For example:

```text
When the Button is pressed,
tell AI.
```

can become a Symphony involving:

```text
Button
AI
```

The Symphony describes their interaction.

## 4.2 Symphony as a Score

Symphony is named after a musical score.

A score coordinates different musicians without requiring every musician to know how the entire piece is performed.

In the same way, a Symphony coordinates different computational selves.

```text
Symphony
├── Button
└── AI
```

The Button performs its part.

The AI performs its part.

The system coordinates them as one composition.

## 4.3 Symphony Is Executable

A Symphony is not simply a description.

It can be executed by **Maxi (MaximalSelf)**.

The human expresses an intent:

```text
Intent
   ↓
AI
   ↓
Symphony
   ↓
Maxi
   ↓
Finite Selves
```

AI produces the Symphony from the human's intent.

Maxi reads its structure and coordinates the participating selves.

## 4.4 Symphony Can Be Composed

A Symphony can involve many selves and many relationships.

For example:

```text
When Button is pressed
within 10 seconds
after Chart crossed SMA
while Workspace is editing
tell AI.
```

This Symphony involves:

```text
Button
Chart
Workspace
AI
```

The individual selves do not need to understand the entire Symphony.

Each receives the part relevant to it.

## 4.5 The Key Idea

> **Symphony is the executable composition of an intent: it brings multiple computational selves together to produce a desired outcome.**
