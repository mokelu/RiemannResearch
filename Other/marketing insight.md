Yes. The insight is basically this:

The Jev ecosystem is currently pulling in two directions. Most people are trying to push Jev toward general-purpose applications: coding agents, automation, software, and other systems. Riemann should not fight them there, because the general Space-Time is still blocked by the program graph.

Instead, Riemann should own the special side, starting with research. The marketing is therefore not “here is what Jev is” or “here is how to program Jev.” It is “here is what you can actually do with this new kind of AI when you put it inside a persistent research world.”

So every piece of content should have three things underneath it: a real research problem, Jev as the AI making or evaluating decisions, and Riemann Research Notebook as the environment where the research happens. Prop-firm challenges, Polymarket, fantasy football, business questions, product research, breaking news, experiments—all of these become different doors into the same product.

And this is how you capture the ecosystem without chasing the ecosystem: you let everyone else talk about what Jev might eventually become, while you demonstrate what it can already become inside a coherent research world.
